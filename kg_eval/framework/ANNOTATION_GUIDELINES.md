# Annotation guidelines: concepts, relations, questions

These guidelines produce the **reference labels** that the KG has never had for Part-of, Application, Similarity and
Extension, and a concept inventory for the textbook. The examples below are *illustrations of the rules*, written for
this document; they are not labels and are never used in evaluation.

Files (generated by `python -m kg_eval.framework.annotation_kit build` and `python -m kg_eval.framework.concepts_eval`):

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `relations/relation_items_BLIND.csv` | one row per concept pair; annotators fill `relation`, `direction`, `confidence_1to3`, `annotator_id`, `notes` |
| `relations/relation_items_KEY_do_not_show_annotators.csv` | where each item came from (predicted / hard negative / pilot / calibration). **Do not open while annotating.** |
| `concepts/sections.md`, `concepts/concept_presence_items.csv` | concept presence in 8 sampled sections |
| `questions/questions_TEMPLATE.csv` | expert-written questions for the downstream gap evaluation |

## 1. General procedure

1. Each annotator copies the blind file (`relation_items_<initials>.csv`) and works **alone**. No KG output, no
   key file, no discussion until both are done.
2. Judge from the textbook sentences shown (`supporting_text_*`, `context_A/B`) **and** standard subject knowledge
   at the level of the textbook. If the relation holds in the subject but the book does not support it, still label
   it, and write `not in text` in `notes`.
3. Pick exactly one `relation`: `Prerequisite`, `Part-of`, `Application`, `Similarity`, `Extension`, `None`, `Unsure`.
   If two seem to hold, choose by the priority Part-of > Extension > Prerequisite > Application > Similarity and
   mention the other one in `notes`.
4. `direction`: `A->B` or `B->A` for directed relations (source -> target as defined below), `undirected` for
   Similarity, `n/a` for None/Unsure. Items present the two concepts in random order, so the column order means nothing.
5. `confidence_1to3`: 1 = guess, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain.
6. Agreement: `python -m kg_eval.framework.annotation_kit agreement A.csv B.csv` (Cohen's kappa on `relation`,
   direction agreement). Target kappa >= 0.6 before adjudication; if lower, revise these guidelines on a 30-item
   practice set (not reused for evaluation) and annotate again.
7. Adjudication: a third person (or both annotators together) resolves every disagreement listed by `agreement`,
   writing `adjudicated_relation`, `adjudicated_direction`, `adjudication_status` (`agreed` / `resolved` / `unresolved`),
   `annotator_id` (e.g. `A1+A2`) and `label_provenance = human`. Unresolved items become `Unsure` and are excluded.
8. Evaluate: `python -m kg_eval.framework.annotation_kit evaluate adjudicated.csv`. The evaluator refuses files that
   are not marked human-labelled.

## 2. Relations (direction convention: source -> target)

### Prerequisite: A -> B
A must be understood **before** B can be understood or used: B's definition, statement or procedure depends on A.
- Positive: "point" -> "line segment" (a segment is defined by two endpoints).
- Negative: two topics taught in the same chapter that do not depend on each other ("kite", "trapezoid").
- Negative: A merely appears earlier in the book.
- Ambiguous: mutual or weak dependence: label the stronger direction if one exists, else `Unsure`.

### Part-of: A -> B
A is a **component** of B (a side, vertex, angle, segment or element that B is made of or contains).
- Positive: "vertex" -> "polygon", "radius" -> "circle", "leg" -> "right triangle".
- Negative: A is a **kind** of B ("right triangle", "triangle"): that is Extension, not Part-of.
- Negative: A is used to compute something about B ("formula", "area"): Application.

### Application: A -> B
A (a formula, theorem, method or concept) is **applied in or used to do** B (a task, procedure or another concept).
- Positive: "Pythagorean theorem" -> "distance formula"; "trigonometric ratios" -> "finding a missing side".
- Negative: A and B only appear in the same sentence.
- Negative: A must be known before B but is not *used* in it: Prerequisite.

### Extension: A -> B
A **builds on, specialises or generalises** B; B is the base concept and comes first (`A extends B`).
Specialisations (A is a special case / kind of B) and generalisations both count; record which in `notes`.
- Positive: "regular polygon" -> "polygon" (special case); "converse of the Pythagorean theorem" -> "Pythagorean theorem".
- Negative: A is a component of B: Part-of.

### Similarity (undirected)
A and B are **conceptually alike or contrasted as alternatives**: same role, analogous definitions, or concepts a
learner is likely to confuse. Write `undirected`.
- Positive: "SSS" and "SAS" (alternative congruence criteria); "sine" and "cosine".
- Negative: geometric **congruence** of two figures is not conceptual similarity between two concepts; the
  geometric relation "similar triangles" is not, by itself, a Similarity relation between the concepts named.
- Negative: one is a kind of the other: Extension.

### None
No relation of these five types, including pairs that only co-occur.

## 3. How items were chosen (for the report, not for annotators)

Predicted edges from three KGs (up to 40 per type, 20 for Prerequisite), 60 **hard negatives** (pairs mentioned in
the same sentence that no KG relates: topically related, so not trivially negative), the 133 pairs of the earlier
single-author `gold_pairs.csv` (re-annotated because it had no guideline, no negatives and inconsistent direction),
and 20 AL-CPL pairs as a calibration check against an independent prerequisite reference. Results computed from the
annotations describe this **sample**, not the whole graph.

## 4. Concept presence (`concepts/`)

For each sampled section, read `sections.md`, then mark every listed term `is_concept` = `yes` / `no` / `unsure`.
A concept is a subject-specific idea a learner must understand (a defined term, figure, property, theorem, formula or
procedure), not a generic word ("example", "measure", "states"). Add concepts that are missing from the list in the
`NEW` rows. Gives concept precision and recall per section sample.

## 5. Questions for the downstream gap evaluation (`questions/`)

A subject expert (teacher) writes 30-50 textbook-level questions and, **independently of the KG**, lists for each the
target concept and the prerequisite concepts a student must know to answer it, with `reference_source` (textbook
section, syllabus or expert judgement). Optional `simulated_known_concepts` defines a simulated learner;
`expected_missing_prerequisites` = expected prerequisites minus known concepts. This tests "estimating potentially
missing prerequisites for a question", not a learner's actual knowledge state.
